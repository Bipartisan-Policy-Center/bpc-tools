How to Read This Chart

What am I looking at?
Each sphere represents one of 472 U.S. occupations. Lines connect occupations that workers frequently move between. Occupations are grouped into 16 communities (clusters) based on how often workers transition between them.
Node size = workforce population
Larger spheres represent occupations with more workers (e.g., Retail Salespersons, Registered Nurses).
Node color = net worker flow
Green = occupation is a net absorber (more workers arriving than leaving)
Red = occupation is a net expeller (more workers leaving than arriving)
Gray = roughly balanced flows
Inflow rate
For each connected occupation, the CPS tells us what percentage of its workers move into this occupation in a typical month. The inflow rate sums those percentages across all connected source occupations—not across time.
Outflow rate
Same logic in reverse: for this occupation, what percentage of its workers leave for each connected destination in a typical month, summed across all connected destinations.
Net flow = inflow − outflow
Positive means more workers are arriving from connected occupations than leaving to them; negative means the opposite. These are average monthly transition rate percentages, not raw worker counts.
Transition volume = inflow + outflow
Total mobility activity. High volume means lots of workers moving in and out of this occupation, regardless of direction.
Communities
Clusters are detected using the Louvain algorithm on the top-4 most frequent career transitions per occupation. Occupations within the same community share more worker flows with each other than with outsiders. Use the dropdown to isolate a community.
Source: Current Population Survey (IPUMS), 2019–2026. Transition rates are monthly averages across matched CPS rotation panels. The original census classification includes 525 occupations; 53 with fewer than 1 million weighted observations were excluded because small samples produce unreliable transition estimates. The remaining 472 occupations cover over 98% of U.S. employment.
